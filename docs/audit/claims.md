What the current implementation supports
The developer has built a Next.js/React cooperative-management application with Supabase records/authentication, wallet access, meter ingestion, dashboards, and Soroban token operations on Stellar Testnet. The docs also describe prospective external buyers, certification, governance and ecosystem integrations. A working internal record/token flow is not evidence that environmental attributes are certified, tradable, eligible for ESG/Scope 2 reporting, or retired in a recognized registry in Argentina.

Evidence used in the table
E1 — Records and inputs. apps/web/app/api/meters/readings/route.ts stores submitted readings as pending. apps/web/app/api/certificates/route.ts:35–87 checks for at least one verified reading in the period, then inserts a caller-supplied total_kwh. It does not derive that total from allocated source readings or reserve their environmental attributes. Input/authorization checks are not independent validation of physical generation.
E2 — Mint and token meaning. apps/web/app/api/mint/route.ts:142–230 mints certificate-flow tokens to the platform minter and stores certificate metadata/transaction links in Supabase. The legacy reading path uses net-injection logic, not the old gross-generation-only behavior. apps/contracts/energy_token/src/lib.rs implements a fungible SEP-41 token: mint/burn arguments are addresses and amounts, not certificate UUIDs, meter proofs, document hashes or registry identifiers. On-chain transaction verification does not verify real-world generation.
E3 — Burn is not purchase or EAC retirement. apps/web/app/api/certificates/retire/route.ts burns from the platform minter, stores caller-supplied buyer details in retirements, and updates database status. This route does not collect/verify payment, transfer a purchased EAC to a buyer, validate reporting eligibility or submit retirement to a recognized EAC registry. A burn reduces token supply; it does not prevent reuse of the same source readings, double issuance or claims in another registry. Successful Testnet transactions alone do not prove an external buyer's entitlement.
E4 — Environmental metrics and purposes. apps/web/app/api/certificates/stats/route.ts multiplies retired kWh by the hardcoded CO2_FACTOR_AR = 0.4; this is arithmetic, not a validated offset methodology. total_kwh_certified sums all certificate rows, including pending ones. The detail page hardcodes ESG/offset/compliance labels; the modal, validation and SQL schema also carry purpose enums. Neither labels nor user-selected purposes establish legal eligibility. Do not treat these totals as independently certified energy or carbon credits.
E5 — P2P mismatch. There is no tracked apps/web/app/marketplace/page.tsx in this snapshot. The award draft, issue generator, legacy simulator and token header still mention marketplace/P2P trading. Generic token transfers/SEP-41 compatibility do not establish Stellar DEX integration, physical delivery or permission to trade electricity in Argentina. The absence of a marketplace UI does not mean contract transfers are impossible.
E6 — Surface and provenance. apps/web/app/page.tsx contains hardcoded Spanish marketing/FAQ copy and illustrative certificate/phone metrics, independent of apps/web/lib/i18n-context.tsx. Also inspected: metadata in app/layout.tsx, login artwork, consumption status text, certificate screens/modals, both sidebars and brand HTML. Editing only the translation dictionary would not address the exposed claims. API tests are mocked and do not establish physical-meter integration or live commercial/registry acceptance.
Priority findings
Public promise exceeds implementation: landing FAQ claims unique assets, documented verification, on-chain identifiers/document references, environmental use and structural elimination of double counting. E1–E3 do not substantiate those promises.
Retirement is overloaded: displayed “bought/sold/retired” copy conflates payment, token burning and recognized EAC retirement. The implementation establishes only the internal record and token-operation path.
ESG/offset/compliance choices: purpose menus and simulated buyer scenarios suggest eligibility without supporting methodology or recognition. Preserve historical records; any later removal of selectable options needs its own coordinated implementation.
Historical P2P and roadmap claims: retain the useful “not a P2P energy market” and “not a formal REC” limitations. Do not present old marketplace plans or future I-REC integration as delivered capabilities.
Argentina legal assertions: categorical “Legal bajo Ley 27.424” / “PSAV: No aplica” statements need qualified legal review, not inference from software architecture.
